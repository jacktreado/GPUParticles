# Adaptive Macro-Step Operator Splitting via Step Doubling

This is a companion to `active_langevin_splitting.md`. There, the macro step $\Delta t$ was chosen by the user and held fixed; only the inner Cash–Karp substep was adaptive. Here we let $\Delta t$ itself become an output of the integrator, controlled by user-supplied tolerances. The structure is almost identical to how Cash–Karp 5(4) controls its own step internally — we just need a bit of care to keep the noise consistent across rejected and accepted trial steps.

---

## 1. Why this needs more thought than an ODE controller

For a deterministic ODE, an embedded RK pair like Cash–Karp 5(4) compares two solutions of orders 4 and 5 starting from the *same* initial state. Their difference is an estimate of the local truncation error, and the PI controller scales $\Delta t$ to keep it near tolerance.

For a stochastic equation, the natural analogue — running two splitting schemes of different orders (Strang vs. Lie–Trotter, or Strang at $\Delta t$ vs. two Strang steps at $\Delta t/2$) — only works if both schemes consume **the same noise**. If we let them draw independent random numbers, the comparison is dominated not by splitting error (which is $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^2)$ for Strang) but by Monte Carlo variance from the Brownian path itself (which is $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\Delta t})$). The error signal is then drowned in noise and the controller sets $\Delta t \to 0$.

The fix is the **Brownian bridge** (and its Ornstein–Uhlenbeck analogue): given the random number used for a coarse step, sample the random numbers for two finer substeps from the *conditional* distribution that reproduces the coarse increment in expectation and gives the correct marginals. This way the comparison reflects splitting error only.

---

## 2. The bridge formulas

We need bridges for the two stochastic subflows: the rotational-diffusion R step and the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck O step. The deterministic D step is, of course, deterministic and gives the same result if we run it twice with the same input — no bridging needed.

### 2.1 Brownian bridge for the R step (2D angle)

The R update over time $\tau$ is

$$
\theta(t+\tau) = \theta(t) + \sqrt{2 D_r \tau}\,\eta, \qquad \eta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1).
$$

Suppose we drew $\eta$ for one big step of size $\tau$ and now want noises $\eta_1, \eta_2$ for two substeps of size $\tau/2$, such that
- each $\eta_a \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ marginally,
- the composite step gives the same final angle as the big step.

The composite step is $\theta(t+\tau) = \theta(t) + \sqrt{D_r \tau}(\eta_1 + \eta_2)$. Matching to $\theta(t) + \sqrt{2 D_r \tau}\,\eta$ gives the constraint $\eta_1 + \eta_2 = \sqrt{2}\,\eta$. The conditional distribution of $(\eta_1, \eta_2)$ given this constraint, with the desired marginals, is

$$
\boxed{\;\eta_1 = \frac{\eta + \tilde\eta}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad \eta_2 = \frac{\eta - \tilde\eta}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad \tilde\eta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\text{ fresh, independent}.\;}
$$

This is exactly the standard Brownian-bridge midpoint construction. Each $\eta_a$ has unit variance, they are independent, and they sum to $\sqrt{2}\eta$ as required. In 3D, apply the same formula component-wise to the Gaussian rotation vector $\boldsymbol{\phi}$.

### 2.2 OU bridge for the O step

The O update over time $\tau$ on a velocity $v$ is

$$
v(t+\tau) = a(\tau)\,v(t) + \sigma(\tau)\,\eta,\qquad a(\tau) = e^{-\gamma\tau/m},\quad \sigma(\tau)^2 = \frac{k_BT}{m}\bigl(1 - a(\tau)^2\bigr).
$$

Two substeps of $\tau/2$ would be

$$
v(t+\tau/2) = \alpha\,v(t) + s\,\eta_1,\qquad v(t+\tau) = \alpha\,v(t+\tau/2) + s\,\eta_2,
$$

with $\alpha = a(\tau/2)$ and $s = \sigma(\tau/2)$. Combining and matching to the one-step result, the constraint becomes

$$
\alpha\,\eta_1 + \eta_2 \;=\; \sqrt{1+\alpha^2}\,\eta.
$$

Imposing $\eta_1, \eta_2 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ independent and conditioning on this constraint:

$$
\boxed{\;\eta_1 = \frac{\alpha\,\eta + \tilde\eta}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^2}},\qquad \eta_2 = \frac{\eta - \alpha\,\tilde\eta}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^2}},\qquad \tilde\eta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\text{ fresh, independent}.\;}
$$

(In the limit $\gamma\to 0$, $\alpha\to 1$ and the OU bridge reduces to the Brownian bridge above; in the strong-friction limit $\alpha\to 0$ and the two halves become essentially independent draws — both as expected.)

These two formulas are all the new machinery we need.

## 3. The adaptive Strang algorithm

### 3.1 One trial step

Given the state $(x, \theta)$ at time $t$ and a trial macro step $\Delta t$, the embedded "5(4)-style" pair is:

**Coarse solution $x^c$:** one Strang step of size $\Delta t$, drawing four independent Gaussians (for the two halved O steps and the two halved R steps).

**Fine solution $x^f$:** two Strang steps of size $\Delta t/2$, with each O-step and R-step noise generated by **bridging** the corresponding coarse noise with a fresh independent Gaussian.

The two solutions agree in the limit $\Delta t \to 0$ for a fixed Brownian path; their difference is a clean estimate of the splitting error:

$$
\mathrm{err} = \|x^f - x^c\|_{\mathrm{wrms}} \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_k\!\left(\frac{x^f_k - x^c_k}{\mathrm{atol} + \mathrm{rtol}\,\max(|x^f_k|,|x^c_k|)}\right)^2}.
$$

This is precisely the same weighted-RMS norm Boost.Odeint uses internally for `make_controlled`.

### 3.2 PI step-size controller

A standard PI controller (the same one Hairer–Nørsett–Wanner recommend for embedded RK pairs) gives

$$
\Delta t_{\mathrm{new}} = \Delta t \cdot \min\!\left(f_{\max},\;\max\!\left(f_{\min},\; S\cdot\mathrm{err}^{-\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{err}_{\mathrm{prev}}^{\beta}\right)\right),
$$

with safety factor $S\approx 0.9$, growth/shrink limits $f_{\max}\approx 5$, $f_{\min}\approx 0.2$, and exponents $\alpha, \beta$ tuned to the order of the *splitting* (not Cash–Karp). Strang has weak order 2, so $\alpha = 1/3$ and $\beta = 1/9$ are reasonable starting values (these are the Gustafsson choices for an order-2 method).

If $\mathrm{err} \le 1$ the step is **accepted** and we use $x^f$ (the more accurate one). If $\mathrm{err} > 1$, the step is **rejected** and we retry at $\Delta t_{\mathrm{new}}$.

### 3.3 Handling rejected steps — the no-skip subtlety

Foster–dos Reis–Strange (2023) showed that the safe way to refine after rejection is **dyadic**: when rejecting at $\Delta t$, the next attempt should be $\Delta t/2$ (not some other shrink factor), and you should keep the noise you already drew at $\Delta t$ — using the bridge to subdivide it consistently. This avoids the Lévy-area bias that can otherwise sneak in.

In practice for additive-noise problems like Langevin dynamics with simple R and O subflows, the bias is empirically small even with the more aggressive PI shrink factors above. A safe pragmatic policy:

- On rejection, store the coarse noises $\eta_O^{(1)}, \eta_O^{(2)}, \eta_R^{(1)}, \eta_R^{(2)}$ that were used for the rejected attempt.
- If the controller wants to retry at $\Delta t/2$ (or some divisor), bridge those stored noises down to the new step size and reuse them.
- If the controller wants a non-dyadic shrink (e.g. $\times 0.7$), either round to the nearest dyadic divisor *or* redraw the noise. The latter introduces the theoretical possibility of a small bias but is often what production codes do, and for additive noise in Langevin dynamics the practical effect is below statistical noise floor for sensible tolerances.
- For Bayesian-sampling / free-energy applications where you really need the exact invariant measure, use only dyadic refinement.

For active matter where you care about MIPS, clustering statistics, transport, etc., the pragmatic policy is essentially always fine.

## 4. C++ implementation

The deterministic flow integration with `make_controlled<runge_kutta_cash_karp54>` and the exact OU / R propagators are all unchanged from the previous document. We add an outer loop that does step doubling, error estimation, and PI control.

```cpp
// adaptive_macro.cpp -- adaptive Δt for the Strang splitting
// (assumes the helper functions / functor from active_langevin_splitting.md)

#include <boost/numeric/odeint.hpp>
#include <random>
#include <vector>
#include <cmath>
#include <algorithm>

namespace odeint = boost::numeric::odeint;
using state_type = std::vector<double>;

// ------------------------------------------------------------------
// Stochastic helper: apply one OU velocity update with externally
// supplied unit-Gaussian draws (so the caller can do bridging).
// ------------------------------------------------------------------
void ou_step_with_noise(state_type& x, double tau, const Params& p,
                        const std::vector<double>& eta /* size 2N */)
{
    const double a = std::exp(-p.gamma * tau / p.m);
    const double sigma = std::sqrt(p.kT / p.m * (1.0 - a*a));
    for (std::size_t i = 0; i < p.N; ++i) {
        x[4*i + 2] = a * x[4*i + 2] + sigma * eta[2*i + 0];
        x[4*i + 3] = a * x[4*i + 3] + sigma * eta[2*i + 1];
    }
}

// ------------------------------------------------------------------
// Same for the rotational R step.
// ------------------------------------------------------------------
void rot_step_with_noise(std::vector<double>& theta, double tau,
                         const Params& p, const std::vector<double>& eta /* size N */)
{
    const double sigma = std::sqrt(2.0 * p.D_r * tau);
    for (std::size_t i = 0; i < p.N; ++i)
        theta[i] += sigma * eta[i];
}

// ------------------------------------------------------------------
// One Strang macro step at size dt, driven by externally supplied
// unit-Gaussian noise vectors. Returns the post-step state.
// ------------------------------------------------------------------
template <class Stepper>
void strang_step_with_noise(Stepper& stepper,
                            state_type& x, std::vector<double>& theta,
                            double t, double dt, const Params& p,
                            const std::vector<double>& etaO1,
                            const std::vector<double>& etaR1,
                            const std::vector<double>& etaR2,
                            const std::vector<double>& etaO2)
{
    ou_step_with_noise(x, 0.5*dt, p, etaO1);
    rot_step_with_noise(theta, 0.5*dt, p, etaR1);

    DeterministicRHS rhs(theta, p);
    odeint::integrate_adaptive(stepper, rhs, x, t, t+dt, dt*0.1);

    rot_step_with_noise(theta, 0.5*dt, p, etaR2);
    ou_step_with_noise(x, 0.5*dt, p, etaO2);
}

// ------------------------------------------------------------------
// Brownian bridge: given the coarse noise eta_coarse (used for one
// step of size tau), produce the two fine-step noises eta_a, eta_b
// for two substeps of size tau/2. tilde is a fresh independent
// unit Gaussian.
// ------------------------------------------------------------------
inline void brownian_bridge(double eta, double tilde,
                            double& eta_a, double& eta_b)
{
    eta_a = (eta + tilde) / std::sqrt(2.0);
    eta_b = (eta - tilde) / std::sqrt(2.0);
}

// ------------------------------------------------------------------
// OU bridge for friction-decay alpha = exp(-gamma*tau/(2m)).
// ------------------------------------------------------------------
inline void ou_bridge(double alpha, double eta, double tilde,
                      double& eta_a, double& eta_b)
{
    const double r = std::sqrt(1.0 + alpha*alpha);
    eta_a = (alpha*eta + tilde) / r;
    eta_b = (eta - alpha*tilde) / r;
}

// ------------------------------------------------------------------
// One adaptive macro step: returns true on accept (state is updated),
// false on reject (state is restored, dt has been shrunk).
// ------------------------------------------------------------------
template <class Stepper>
bool adaptive_strang_step(Stepper& stepper,
                          state_type& x, std::vector<double>& theta,
                          double t, double& dt, double& dt_prev_accepted,
                          double& err_prev,
                          const Params& p, std::mt19937_64& rng,
                          double atol, double rtol)
{
    const std::size_t Nv = 2*p.N;       // size of velocity-noise vectors
    const std::size_t Nr = p.N;         // size of rotational-noise vectors
    std::normal_distribution<double> N01(0.0, 1.0);

    auto draw = [&](std::size_t n) {
        std::vector<double> v(n);
        for (auto& x_ : v) x_ = N01(rng);
        return v;
    };

    // 1. Draw coarse-step noise (used by the Δt trial).
    auto etaO1c = draw(Nv), etaO2c = draw(Nv);
    auto etaR1c = draw(Nr), etaR2c = draw(Nr);

    // 2. Coarse trial: copy state, run one Strang step at dt.
    state_type x_c = x;
    std::vector<double> theta_c = theta;
    strang_step_with_noise(stepper, x_c, theta_c, t, dt, p,
                           etaO1c, etaR1c, etaR2c, etaO2c);

    // 3. Fine trial: bridge each coarse noise into two halves, run two
    //    Strang steps of dt/2.
    const double alpha = std::exp(-p.gamma * dt / (4.0 * p.m));
    //                          ^ note: half of the *half-step* tau = dt/2,
    //                            so the bridge scale is exp(-γ·(dt/2)/(2m))
    //                                                  = exp(-γ·dt/(4m)).

    // bridge noise containers
    std::vector<double> etaO1a(Nv), etaO1b(Nv), etaO2a(Nv), etaO2b(Nv);
    std::vector<double> etaR1a(Nr), etaR1b(Nr), etaR2a(Nr), etaR2b(Nr);

    auto tildeO1 = draw(Nv), tildeO2 = draw(Nv);
    auto tildeR1 = draw(Nr), tildeR2 = draw(Nr);

    for (std::size_t k = 0; k < Nv; ++k) {
        ou_bridge(alpha, etaO1c[k], tildeO1[k], etaO1a[k], etaO1b[k]);
        ou_bridge(alpha, etaO2c[k], tildeO2[k], etaO2a[k], etaO2b[k]);
    }
    for (std::size_t k = 0; k < Nr; ++k) {
        brownian_bridge(etaR1c[k], tildeR1[k], etaR1a[k], etaR1b[k]);
        brownian_bridge(etaR2c[k], tildeR2[k], etaR2a[k], etaR2b[k]);
    }

    state_type x_f = x;
    std::vector<double> theta_f = theta;
    strang_step_with_noise(stepper, x_f, theta_f, t, 0.5*dt, p,
                           etaO1a, etaR1a, etaR2a, etaO2a);
    strang_step_with_noise(stepper, x_f, theta_f, t+0.5*dt, 0.5*dt, p,
                           etaO1b, etaR1b, etaR2b, etaO2b);

    // 4. Weighted-RMS error norm (positions + velocities).
    double err2 = 0.0;
    for (std::size_t k = 0; k < x.size(); ++k) {
        const double sc = atol + rtol * std::max(std::fabs(x_c[k]), std::fabs(x_f[k]));
        const double d = (x_f[k] - x_c[k]) / sc;
        err2 += d*d;
    }
    for (std::size_t k = 0; k < theta.size(); ++k) {
        const double sc = atol + rtol;     // angle scale ~ O(1)
        const double d = (theta_f[k] - theta_c[k]) / sc;
        err2 += d*d;
    }
    const double err = std::sqrt(err2 / (x.size() + theta.size()));

    // 5. PI controller -- Strang is weak order 2, so use exponent 1/3.
    constexpr double S      = 0.9;       // safety factor
    constexpr double f_max  = 5.0;       // max step growth
    constexpr double f_min  = 0.2;       // max step shrink
    constexpr double alpha_ = 1.0/3.0;   // PI proportional exponent
    constexpr double beta_  = 1.0/9.0;   // PI integral exponent

    if (err <= 1.0) {
        // accept fine solution
        x = std::move(x_f);
        theta = std::move(theta_f);
        const double scale = (err > 1e-12)
            ? S * std::pow(err, -alpha_) * std::pow(err_prev, beta_)
            : f_max;
        dt_prev_accepted = dt;
        err_prev = std::max(err, 1e-4);   // floor avoids runaway growth
        dt *= std::clamp(scale, f_min, f_max);
        return true;
    } else {
        // reject -- shrink and retry
        const double scale = S * std::pow(err, -alpha_);
        dt *= std::clamp(scale, f_min, 1.0);   // never grow on reject
        return false;
    }
}

// ------------------------------------------------------------------
// Outer driver loop
// ------------------------------------------------------------------
int main()
{
    Params p;
    /* ... fill in as before ... */

    state_type x(4*p.N);
    std::vector<double> theta(p.N);
    std::mt19937_64 rng(12345);

    auto stepper = odeint::make_controlled(
        1.0e-8, 1.0e-6,
        odeint::runge_kutta_cash_karp54<state_type>()
    );

    // user-facing tolerances for the macro adaptive loop
    const double atol_macro = 1.0e-4;
    const double rtol_macro = 1.0e-3;

    // bookkeeping
    double dt = 1.0e-3;                    // initial guess only
    double dt_prev_accepted = dt;
    double err_prev = 1.0;                 // PI memory

    // safety bound: never let dt exceed 0.1 of fastest stochastic time
    const double dt_max = 0.1 * std::min(p.m / p.gamma, 1.0 / p.D_r);

    double t = 0.0;
    const double T_end = 100.0;
    while (t < T_end) {
        dt = std::min(dt, std::min(dt_max, T_end - t));
        const bool accepted = adaptive_strang_step(
            stepper, x, theta, t, dt, dt_prev_accepted, err_prev,
            p, rng, atol_macro, rtol_macro);
        if (accepted) t += dt_prev_accepted;
        // ... observers, neighbour-list updates, etc ...
    }
    return 0;
}
```

What changed compared to the fixed-step version:

1. The `_with_noise` variants of the OU and R step take their Gaussian draws as arguments instead of generating them internally. This lets us pass either freshly-drawn noise (coarse trial) or bridged noise (fine trial).
2. `brownian_bridge` and `ou_bridge` implement the formulas from §2 in three lines each.
3. `adaptive_strang_step` does the doubling, error estimation, and PI control. The Cash–Karp inner stepper is unchanged; `integrate_adaptive` is just called twice per macro step (once for the coarse trial, twice for the fine trial — three deterministic flow integrations total).

The cost per accepted macro step is roughly 3× a fixed-step macro step (one coarse + two fine deterministic integrations). In return you get a $\Delta t$ that automatically tracks the difficulty of the problem: when nothing interesting is happening it grows toward `dt_max`, and when activity drives particles into close LJ encounters it shrinks. Empirically the average step is often comparable to what you would have set by hand for the worst-case region, but the integrator handles transient stiff phases without you having to think about them.

## 5. When this is worth it

The adaptive macro step pays off when:

- Activity is high enough that close LJ encounters are common (and dynamic — they vary in time and position).
- You don't know a priori what the right $\Delta t$ should be (e.g. parameter sweeps over $f_a$, $D_r$, density).
- You want the simulation to remain accurate even when an unusually energetic event happens (cluster fragmentation, jet formation, …).

It's *not* worth the extra complexity when:

- Your simulation is in a quasi-steady regime with bounded interaction strength.
- You're doing equilibrium Bayesian sampling and need a known invariant measure.
- The deterministic forces are mild (then Cash–Karp inner control does little anyway).

For most active-matter exploration, the answer is "yes, this is worth it". For a tightly-controlled production benchmark, fixed $\Delta t$ is more reproducible and easier to reason about.

## 6. Two further refinements

**a) Re-use the inner Cash–Karp work.** The coarse trial integrates D over $[t, t+\Delta t]$. The fine trial integrates D over $[t, t+\Delta t/2]$ then $[t+\Delta t/2, t+\Delta t]$. With minor surgery (saving and restoring states), Cash–Karp's accepted internal steps can be partially reused, cutting the cost of the trial from 3× to ~2×. Boost.Odeint's `dense_output_runge_kutta` provides the bookkeeping if you want to push this.

**b) Use the inner Cash–Karp's average step as a hint for $\Delta t$.** A simpler heuristic that avoids step doubling entirely: at the end of each macro step, ask the controlled stepper what the average inner $\delta t$ was. If $\langle\delta t\rangle \ll \Delta t$, the deterministic flow was stiff this step; consider shrinking $\Delta t$. If $\langle\delta t\rangle \approx \Delta t$, the deterministic flow was easy; consider growing $\Delta t$. This conflates deterministic stiffness with stochastic accuracy — strictly speaking the wrong signal — but it is much cheaper and works surprisingly well as long as your macro $\Delta t$ stays well below $\min(m/\gamma, 1/D_r)$ as a hard upper bound. I'd recommend it as a quick-and-dirty first pass before investing in the full step-doubling machinery.
